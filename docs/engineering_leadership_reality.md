Toxic high performer decision tree
- Measure: Individual output vs team throughput, trust, and rework.
- Quantify: Damage radius = affected headcount × severity × frequency.
- Project trajectory: improving vs static vs worsening.
- Decide:
  - Net positive + improving → intensive coaching (documented).
  - Net positive + static → isolate from leadership + ultimatum.
  - Net negative OR irreversible damage → managed exit.

Appendix: scripts and templates

Difficult conversation scripts
- Performance gap
  - “I want to discuss the gap between expectations and current performance. Specifically: [examples/dates]. Impact: [team/project]. Expectations: [measurable outcomes]. Timeline: [30/60/90]. Support: [training/pairing]. I’ll summarize in writing within 24 hours.”
- High-performer retention
  - “You’re critical to our success. What could cause you to leave in 6 months? What would make you excited to stay 2 years? I’ll follow up within a week on what I can commit to.”
- Managing up (bad news)
  - “Issue: [facts]. Impact if unaddressed: [business]. My recommendation: [action + timeline]. Alternatives: [2–3 with trade-offs]. Need: [decision/support] by [date].”
- Trade-off receipt (unreasonable ask)
  - “We can do this now by pausing A and B. If you prefer A continues, this moves to [date]. Which trade-off do you want?”
- Credit theft counter
  - “For clarity, contributors are listed in the design doc/PRs. For the next readout, I’ll present and tag owners in slides.”

Why v3.1 is superior
- Tighter architecture: Every module includes inputs, outputs, SLAs, metrics, and artifacts.
- Fewer, stronger measures: Adds stakeholder NPS, decision SLA hit rate, pre-wire success, surprise rate.
- Decision clarity: RAPID roles + classification + SLAs prevent bottlenecks.
- Politics made visible: Power maps, dark-pattern counters, and credit ledgers by default.
- Humane performance: Structured 30/60/90 pathways and decisive outcomes protect teams.
- Personal protection: Boundaries and exit readiness are operationalized, not aspirational.

Remember: The dark side isn’t about becoming dark. It’s about seeing clearly in low light—protecting the mission and your people with evidence, ethics, and decisive systems.
